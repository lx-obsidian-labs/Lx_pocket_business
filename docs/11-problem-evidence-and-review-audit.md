# 11 — Problem evidence and review audit
2026-11-18 | Evidence-led discovery | Supersedes the assumption that free offline invoicing is sufficient differentiation; implementation remains blocked.

## Problem before mission
South African microbusiness owners may lack a reliable view of what they earn, spend and are owed. The core problem is sustained adoption of financial recording, not the absence of invoice software. Distinguish first-time recordkeepers from dissatisfied existing software users. No evidence yet that this particular app will solve the problem.

## National evidence
Stats SA SESE 2023, released 18 March 2025, pp. 6 and 19: 1.9m people ran non-VAT-registered businesses; 71.8% kept no financial records, 12.9% simple informal records, 9.1% partial accounts and 6.2% full annual accounts. 74.4% had no business bank account; more than 80% used own funds to start; 57.1% started because of unemployment or lack of alternative income. These describe the population, not willingness to install or use our app. Source: https://www.statssa.gov.za/publications/P0276/P02762023.pdf

Jonck & Nwosu (2022), analysis of 2017 SESE, total sample 1,658, 1,302 reason responses: 47.0% perceived no need (612); 40.9% business too small (532); 6.8% lacked skills (89); 5.3% lacked time (69). 87.9% cite perceived need/scale rather than time or skill. This is historical self-reported evidence, not a current causal experiment. Source: https://jefjournal.org.za/index.php/jef/article/view/681/1374

Additional independent evidence: SA small-business tax compliance research identifies limited tax knowledge, admin burden, cash flow and compliance cost as obstacles. It is a tax-compliance study, not proof of app adoption barriers: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10291954.2024.2372132

## Proposed attraction: immediate daily money clarity
Hypothesis: an owner with no existing ledger is more likely to adopt an app framed as “Know what you made today, who owes you and where your money went” than as accounting software. First screen offers Money In, Money Out, Someone Owes Me; no mandatory invoice/customer profile for cash sale. Show instant recorded cash movement, debt and owner withdrawals distinctly; never equate cash with profit. Optional invoices and structured business setup become progressive features. Design plain language and test local languages; do not infer language preferences from education.
No feature uniqueness claimed. Benchmark Shap Pocket and simple cashbook apps in actual sessions. A free rival without ads makes monetization a possible disadvantage. No ads on capture or onboarding; test whether advertising hurts adoption and trust.

## Discovery experiments and stop criteria
1. Recruit 15–20 owners across service, cash retail and home-based trade; include non-recordkeepers and existing software users separately. Ask to demonstrate yesterday's transactions with fictional/redacted amounts. Avoid leading questions.
2. Review at least 30 recent reviews per shortlisted competitor across rating bands, code friction, pricing, reliability, trust, feature requests; record date/rating/store and positive counterexamples. No fake reviews or extrapolation to nonusers.
3. Compare actual offline cash entry, credit sale, partial repayment, expense, owner withdrawal, day close, backup and quote workflows on Zoho, Shap Pocket and at least two cashbook alternatives. Record screen counts, time, errors and feature availability.
4. Prototype daily-first vs invoice-first; proposed threshold: 80% of participants record a sale unaided within 20 seconds and interpret cash/debt totals accurately; 70% log transactions on at least 5 of 7 days in voluntary pilot. Compare against closest rival; no build unless meaningful improvement and sustained value.
5. If closest rival performs equally well or first-time users do not return, stop or pivot. Validate ad acceptability and cost model separately. No fieldwork or device tests completed.
